Weather & Dress Code

Early May is one of the most beautiful times to visit South Korea. You can expect mild, pleasant spring weather, with daytime temperatures generally hovering around 18–22°C. Evenings cool down to around 10–13°C, so a light jacket or wrap is recommended for the afterparty. Historically, early May offers clear, sunny skies with very little chance of rain.

For the celebration, we kindly request formal or semi-formal attire—suits for gentlemen and elegant dresses or equivalent for ladies. Please note that the traditional hanok venue features natural stone and wooden pathways, so we highly recommend choosing comfortable footwear for the day.

Exploring Seoul

Getting Around

  • Navigate like a Local: Google Maps does not provide walking directions in Korea. We highly recommend downloading Naver Map (or KakaoMap) before you arrive—it's essential for navigating the city!
  • T-Money Card: The easiest way to pay for public transit. You can buy and top up a T-Money card (cash only) at any convenience store like CU, GS25, or 7-Eleven.
  • Airport to City: Trains (AREX) and Airport Limousine buses are excellent ways to get from Incheon Airport to Seoul. However, because local cards are often needed at automated kiosks, buying your initial tickets at the manned airport counters with cash or a credit card might be easiest.
  • Taxis: Uber works perfectly in Seoul. It automatically dispatches local "UT" taxis and charges your linked card, skipping any language or payment barriers entirely.

Sights & Culture

  • Palaces & Hanboks: A visit to Gyeongbokgung Palace is highly recommended. If you rent and wear a traditional hanbok from one of the nearby rental shops, your entry to the palace is completely free! (Be sure to check opening hours, as palaces often close on Tuesdays).
  • Hanok Village: Wander through the beautifully preserved Bukchon Hanok Village to see hundreds of traditional Korean homes nestled right in the middle of the modern city.
  • Namsan Seoul Tower: For breathtaking views of the massive cityscape, head up to the base level of Namsan Tower. The viewing decks at the base are free and offer a stunning panorama, especially at sunset.

Food & Drink

  • Korean BBQ & Delicacies: Korean Barbecue is an absolute must, and fantastic restaurants are incredibly easy to find on almost every corner. Don’t miss out on trying other local favorites like Korean fried chicken, bibimbap, and street food.
  • Cafe Culture: Seoul’s cafe scene is legendary. Amazing breakfast and brunch cafes are tucked into every neighborhood. Just use Naver Map to search "Cafe" around your location, or explore trendy areas like Seongsu-dong or Yeonnam-dong.

Shopping & Nightlife

  • Myeongdong Shopping: Myeongdong is shopping heaven. Visit the massive Lotte Department Store and the surrounding streets. Remember to carry your physical passport—many stores offer immediate tax-free discounts right at the checkout.
  • Itaewon: For nightlife and partying, the vibrant streets behind the Hamilton Hotel in Itaewon are famous for a fantastic mix of bars, lounges, and clubs.

Helpful Extras

  • Translation: Download the Papago app for the most accurate Korean-to-English translations (far superior to Google Translate here).
  • Tipping: There is absolutely no tipping in South Korea. It is not expected in restaurants, taxis, or hotels!
